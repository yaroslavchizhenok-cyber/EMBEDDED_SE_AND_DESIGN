# Swarm Parade: Five Drones, One Clock
## SOC3050 ARM Edition — Part 3, Applications

**Reference**: [STM32 Reference Manual](https://www.st.com/resource/en/reference_manual/rm0490-stm32c0x1-advanced-armbased-32bit-mcus-stmicroelectronics.pdf) ·
**Board manual**: [UM2953, STM32 Nucleo-64 boards (MB1717)](https://www.st.com/resource/en/user_manual/um2953-stm32c0-nucleo64-board-mb1717-stmicroelectronics.pdf)

**Lesson 14 flew one drone. This one flies five, in formation.**

A drone light show is not swarm intelligence. Every drone follows its own
timed track, one ground station keeps them on one clock, and the spacing is
proved safe before anything takes off. In this lesson the chip does all of
it: it plans a show, refuses it if two drones would ever come closer than
3 m, flies five copies of a flight computer in gusty wind, and scores the
result. The station on the PC draws it in 3D.

Figures marked **measured on the host** come from `host\test.bat`, which runs
the firmware's own C on the PC. Nothing in this deck has been watched
running in Wokwi yet; the last slide says what that leaves open.

## The Board: This Lesson's Pins

Before any code, find the pins. Every highlighted dot is a header pin that
this lesson's `diagram.json` wires something to, at its position on the
Nucleo-C031C6, to scale. A label reads *MCU pin (header name, if
different)* and what the pin does here.

The board is the shared app board, unchanged, so the OLED (PB8/PB9) and the
buzzer (PA6) are still wired. **This firmware uses neither:** slide 10 says
why.

## The Chip: The Same Pins, as Port Bits

position in its port's registers**. Pin *n* of GPIOA is bit *n* of
`GPIOA->IDR`, bits 2*n*+1 : 2*n* of `GPIOA->MODER`, and so on (lesson 05,
slide 4). Each cell below is one pin, drawn in register order, marked with
how this lesson configures it.

## Slide 2: Why It Runs Like a Real Light Show

| real light show | this lesson |
|---|---|

## Slide 3: From One Drone to Five

Lesson 14 kept its one drone in a file-level variable:

```c
static fc_t fc;                      /* lesson 14: THE drone */
fc_t *fc_get(void) { return &fc; }
```

A swarm cannot work that way. Here every function takes the drone it works
on, and the file holds no state at all:

```c
void pilot_step(pilot_t *p, const meas_t *m, cmd_t *out);   /* pilot.c */

static drone_t fleet[FLEET_MAX];     /* fleet.c: five airframes + five pilots */
for (int i = 0; i < count; i++) {
    craft_sense(&fleet[i].body, &seen);
    pilot_step(&fleet[i].brain, &seen, &fleet[i].asked);
}
```

That is the usual C way to write an object: the struct is the object, and
the pointer is `this`. `fleet_step()` moves **all** drones one 10 ms tick,
so none can run ahead of another.

## Slide 4: A Moving Setpoint Needs Feed-Forward

Lesson 14 flew "to the waypoint at cruise speed", so its arrival time
depended on the wind. A formation needs each drone **at its slot at a given
moment**. So the pilot is given the goal's position *and its velocity*:

```c
want.x = p->goal_v.x + cx;     /* feed-forward: fly WITH the goal              */
                               /* cx = k_pos * (goal - estimate), capped at 2.5 m/s */
```

Without the first term, a drone chases a moving goal from behind, `goal_v /
k_pos` metres late, in every move.

**Measured on the host** (`ff off`): the worst tracking error goes from
**0.58 m to 5.42 m**.

---

## Slide 5: Who Goes Where — the Hungarian Algorithm

Five drones, five slots: 120 ways to pair them. `plan_assign()` picks the
pairing with the smallest **sum of squared distances** (Kuhn 1955, Munkres
1957), in O(n³).

Why *squared*? Turpin, Michael and Kumar ("CAPT", 2014) proved that with
this choice, **straight paths flown in step do not cross**, provided both
formations are spaced at about 1.4 × the safety distance. With plain
distances, two long paths may swap and cross.

**Measured on the host:** 300 random cases, every one equal to brute force
over all permutations.

---

## Slide 6: How Fast — One Profile for Everyone

The longest move gets a **trapezoidal** speed profile: speed up at `amax`,
cruise at `vmax`, brake at `amax`. Every other drone flies **the same
profile, scaled to its own distance**:

```
distance done = fraction(t) x my path length       (the same fraction for all)
```

So all five start together and arrive together, and the formation keeps
its shape on the way. A steep path stretches the profile further, so that
nobody climbs faster than `vzmax`.

---

## Slide 7: Check, Repair, or Refuse

Before take-off `plan.c` samples every pair, every 0.2 s, through the whole
show.

- **Too close in a formation** (two slots < `sep`): **refused**. The serial
  monitor names the formation and the two drones: *"small: slots 0 and 1 are
  2.35 m apart (sep 3.00) - make it bigger"*.
- **Too close in a move:** **repaired**. One of the pair flies over or under
  the other (up, across, down), and the move is timed again. Light shows
  call this using layers.
- **No repair works:** **refused**.

**Measured on the host:** the parade needs no repair. A row of five turning
into a column across it needs 9, and its closest planned pass is then
3.00 m.

## Slide 8: The Director — One Clock

`director_tick()`, every 10 ms of show time:

1. every 100 ms: each drone's target = the plan at *t*, position **and**
   velocity;
2. `fleet_step()`: every drone, one tick;
3. score: each drone's distance from the plan; the closest pair;
4. one telemetry line.

| state | |
|---|---|
| WARMUP | on the pads, GPS settling, 2 s |
| READY | waiting for `a` |
| FLYING / LANDING | the show; LANDING once told to land, or after `abort` |
| DONE | the score is out; `a` flies again |
| REFUSED | the plan failed its check |

## Slide 9: Three Tasks, and Why `fly` Is the Lowest

| task | prio | does |
|---|---|---|
| `link` | 3 | the shell, the station's keys |
| `ui` | 2 | buttons, knob, LD4, every 20 ms |
| `fly` | **1** | `director_tick()` |

In Wokwi one tick of five drones can take **longer than 10 ms**, and then `fly`
never sleeps. Lesson 13 froze because a high-priority task that never sleeps
starves everything below it. At the **bottom**, `fly` gets only the time the
others leave: the show goes into **slow motion**, and the buttons and the
shell keep working. On silicon it sleeps until each 10 ms is due.

**One telemetry line per tick:** the seven lines of a report are about
390 B, and `uart.c`'s transmit ring holds 256. Sent at once, the last drones'
lines would never fit.

## Slide 10: Fitting in 32 KB

The first build: **43 192 B of flash for a 32 KB chip.**

| cut | saved |
|---|---|
| `strtok()` → a ten-line word splitter: newlib's calls `assert()`, which prints through all of stdio | ~3.5 KB |
| `$KEY` presses read in `Main.c`, not via `kbd.c` + `keys.c` | ~1.4 KB |
| `fmt.c`, a small `snprintf` for integers and strings | ~2 KB |
| **no OLED**: driver, I²C, picture, station mirror | ~4 KB |
| sin/cos/sqrt compiled once (`trig.c`) | ~0.7 KB |
| `softfloat.c` instead of libgcc's float routines - and twice as fast | ~2.8 KB |

Now **29 868 B (91.2 %)**, RAM 10 696 B of 12 KB. The OLED stays dark, and
LD4 blinks instead. The lesson: on a small chip, one library call can cost
more than your whole algorithm. **Read the map file.**
